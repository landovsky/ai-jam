# **The AI Jam: Community README**

### **1. The Heart: We Grab the Wave Together**

The AI Jam exists for people who *know* they need to catch the AI wave — and are ready to invest real effort into it. We don't debate whether AI matters. We build, experiment, break things, and share what we learn.

This is not a lecture hall. This is not a support group. This is a living lab where people at different levels push each other forward — by doing, not just talking.

### **2. What We Believe**

- **Ownership is shared.** Nobody owns the AI Jam. It's an open community with shared responsibility. We co-create the program, co-facilitate the sessions, and co-shape where this goes.
- **Explaining is learning.** When you articulate what you know to someone a level below you, you clarify your own thinking. Teaching is not charity — it's one of the most underappreciated ways to grow.
- **Diversity makes us stronger.** A room full of identical skill levels is boring. We want the full spectrum — from people just finding their footing to those pushing the bleeding edge. The magic happens in the gaps between levels.
- **Personal responsibility comes first.** You choose your topic, your group, your level of engagement. If something isn't working for you, it's on you to move — not on everyone else to slow down.

### **3. Who Is This For?**

**The mindset matters more than the skill level.**

You belong here if:

- You *know* AI is where you need to be and you're ready to invest in getting there.
- You want to apply AI to real problems — your work, your projects, your life.
- You're willing to contribute, not just consume. Bring a topic, facilitate a discussion, share an experiment.
- You treat commitments seriously — when you say you'll come, you come.

You might not be the right fit if:

- You're still debating whether AI is worth your time.
- You want to sit back and absorb without contributing anything.
- You're looking for a beginner course that starts from zero.

**Upper end: unlimited.** The most advanced people are always welcome — we actively want them. They raise the bar for everyone.

**Lower end: you need the spark.** You don't need to be technical. But you need the drive — the conviction that this matters and the willingness to invest your energy. We'll meet you where you are, as long as you're moving.

### **4. How We Jam (The Format)**

We use a **barcamp-style format** — topics are collected in advance, and people self-select into groups based on what interests them.

1. **Lightning Talks (30 min):** Quick-fire presentations. Five minutes each, strictly timeboxed. Show what you've been working on — breakthroughs *and* dead ends.
2. **Breakout Sessions:** Small groups form around topics. Some are deeply technical (code flying, tools running). Some are exploratory (use cases, workflows, strategy). You pick where you go.
3. **Regroup & Share:** Groups reconvene and share key takeaways — distilled to the essentials so everyone benefits, regardless of which group they were in.

The breakout structure is key. It means:
- Advanced people can go deep without being slowed down.
- People earlier in their journey have a space that matches their pace.
- Nobody is stuck in a session that doesn't serve them — you move with your feet.

We also run **hackathons** — longer, output-driven sessions where the goal is to walk away with something tangible. Code, a workflow, a prototype. Not just ideas.

### **5. The Social Contract**

- **RSVP means RSVP.** When you confirm, we count on you. Flaking erodes the group. If something comes up, tell us early — but make it the exception, not the pattern.
- **Contribute, don't just consume.** Over time, every member should bring topics, facilitate discussions, or share experiments. Passive attendance has a shelf life.
- **Respect the room.** When a group goes technical, let them go technical. When someone is learning, acknowledge them. We don't slow the fast ones down, and we don't leave the new ones invisible.
- **No gatekeeping, no hand-holding.** We welcome people in — but we don't spoon-feed. You're responsible for your own growth. We'll give you the environment; you bring the effort.

### **6. The "Recipe" Concept**

When someone solves a problem, we document it as a **Recipe**:

- **The Problem:** What were you trying to do?
- **The Stack:** What tools did you use?
- **The Prompt/Workflow:** The actual steps.
- **The Verdict:** Did it work? What surprised you?

Recipes are shared so the community builds a library of real, reproducible knowledge — not theory, but tested approaches.

### **7. The Price of Admission**

There is no fee. The currency is **effort and engagement**.

To stay in the loop, you contribute — a recipe, an experiment, a topic to explore, or help with organizing. We trade participation for participation.

This ensures that everyone in the room is a player, not a spectator.
